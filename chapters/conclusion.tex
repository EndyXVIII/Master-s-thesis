\chapter{Conclusions}
\label{ch:conclusions}

This thesis investigated whether 3D geometric structures and natural language representations share sufficient topological homology to be aligned without extensive supervision. The primary motivation was practical: large-scale paired 3D-text datasets are prohibitively expensive to construct, rendering any method capable of bypassing this requirement highly valuable. While the empirical results did not yield a conventional algorithmic success, they provided something arguably more critical: a rigorous diagnostic mapping of precisely where and why zero-shot geometric alignment fails, offering profound insights into the latent spaces that independent neural networks actually construct.

\section{Summary of Results}
\label{sec:summary}

The empirical findings establish a stark dichotomy between supervised and unsupervised alignment paradigms. On one hand, supervised methods performed robustly. Canonical Correlation Analysis (CCA) paired with an affine transformation --- the linear baseline replicated from the foundational literature --- achieved a $35.7\%$ Top-5 retrieval accuracy on the PointNet and \gls{clip} pairings. Riemannian Metric Learning (RML), introduced in this work as an original non-linear extension, followed closely at $27.2\%$. However, both methods are fundamentally constrained by their reliance on $30{,}000$ explicitly labelled anchor pairs to optimize the mapping.

Conversely, the unsupervised approaches failed to generalize. Across five distinct \gls{ot} variants --- \gls{sgw}, \gls{risgw}, \gls{ssrisgw}, \gls{fgw}, and \gls{lrgw} --- no purely unsupervised method exceeded a $3.2\%$ Top-5 accuracy. Even Fused GW, which incorporates a limited degree of anchor supervision, peaked at $4.4\%$ --- approximately $8\times$ below the fully supervised \gls{cca}+Affine baseline. As demonstrated by the thirteen-test ablation study, this degradation was not the result of stochastic variance, sub-optimal hyperparameters, or implementation errors. Instead, the conclusion is definitive: PointNet and \gls{clip}, trained on disparate sensory modalities without a shared objective, do not naturally converge to structurally isometric spaces. Forcing an unsupervised solver to minimise the structural discrepancy between non-isometric manifolds does not approximate a valid alignment; it actively scrambles the semantic correspondences that do exist. This fundamental geometric incompatibility renders zero-shot relational alignment unfeasible in this context.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

While the diagnostic conclusions drawn from this research are definitive for the evaluated spaces, several limitations define the scope of this work.

The primary limitation is computational scale. Exact Gromov-Wasserstein optimisation scales at $\mathcal{O}(n^3)$, precluding the computation of dense transport plans across the entire dataset. As detailed in Section~\ref{subsec:fused_gw}, this computational bottleneck necessitated restricting the unsupervised and semi-supervised GW variants to $1{,}000$--$5{,}000$ anchors (compared to the $30{,}000$ utilized for \gls{cca}+Affine) and required the use of low-rank approximations and sliced 1D projections. While it is theoretically possible that these relaxations obscured a viable global minimum, the empirical evidence suggests otherwise. The fine-grained epsilon sweeps and the anchor-count grid searches revealed no performance gains beyond $n{=}5{,}000$ samples, and the perfectly flat cost landscape observed in Tests 3 and 13 strongly indicates the absence of a superior latent alignment. Nonetheless, because these approximations inherently smooth the transport matrix, the possibility that fine-grained, localized topological structures were averaged out cannot be entirely dismissed.

A secondary limitation concerns the linguistic distribution of the dataset. The Objaverse dataset lacks human-annotated captions, relying instead on the synthetic Cap3D framework. Consequently, the text encoders were aligned against highly descriptive, formulaic captions rather than the semantic variability inherent to natural human language. The extent to which this synthetic linguistic structure impacts cross-modal isometry remains an open question in its own right.

Finally, the evaluations were largely restricted to pre-trained, frozen feature extractors, with one deliberate exception: SparseConv was executed utilizing random, untrained weights (Section~\ref{subsec:3d_encoders}) to isolate the baseline spatial covariance of the architecture from learned representations. Furthermore, while \gls{clip} possesses extensive generalized visual grounding, it was not natively exposed to 3D voxel or point-cloud spatial logic during its foundational training. Whether a 3D backbone imbued with explicit geometric priors --- possessing greater structural awareness than PointNet's global pooling mechanism --- might narrow the isometry gap remains outside the scope of this thesis.

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:future_work}

The theoretical bottleneck exposed by this thesis provides clear directions for future multimodal research. Attempting post-hoc, zero-shot geometric alignment on independently trained, unaligned spaces is highly likely to remain a theoretical dead end. Future efforts should instead pivot toward the following paradigms:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Joint Contrastive Pre-training:} Rather than attempting to align disparate spaces after they have been frozen, architectures should be trained concurrently using contrastive objectives. By penalizing non-isometric topologies directly within the loss function during the initial training phase, the resulting latent spaces become inherently co-located, rendering complex post-hoc optimal transport unnecessary.
    
    \item \textbf{Relaxed Topological Constraints:} If post-hoc alignment is strictly required, future \gls{ot} solvers must abandon the strict global isometry assumption. Research could explore highly localized, partial transport mechanisms or heavily regularised variants of Fused GW that incorporate weak heuristic priors --- such as coarse categorical clustering --- to softly guide the transport plan without necessitating tens of thousands of exact instance-level pairings.
    
    \item \textbf{LLMs as Explicit Spatial Bridges:} Instead of mapping raw geometry directly to continuous text embeddings, intermediate discrete representations could be utilized. Modern Large Language Models could be prompted to generate highly structured, procedural descriptions of 3D objects (e.g., scene graphs or bounding-box relationships). Aligning 3D geometry to this explicit spatial logic may exhibit a significantly higher degree of structural isomorphism than aligning it to standard descriptive captions.

    \item \textbf{Subspace Blind Matching on Semantic Categories:} Building upon the exact quadratic assignment evaluation, future research could evaluate matching accuracy by applying the 10-category blind match protocol directly within the low-dimensional embedding. By leaving the underlying feature extraction and projection pipeline completely untouched, this approach would isolate whether the noise-filtering properties of the shared subspace sufficiently regularize the geometry to facilitate highly accurate, training-free semantic matching at the categorical level.
\end{itemize}

In conclusion, while the absolute structural alignment of 3D and textual modalities via unsupervised optimal transport remains elusive, mapping the precise theoretical boundaries of this failure has yielded invaluable insights. By systematically eliminating methodological artifacts, this thesis clarifies the topological behavior of independent neural representations, establishing a rigorous foundation for the development of more robust, geometrically aware multimodal architectures.
