\chapter*{Summary}
\markboth{Summary}{Summary}

\begingroup
\setlength{\parindent}{0pt}
\setlength{\parskip}{0.8em}

Over the past several years, multimodal artificial intelligence has seen a substantial rise in its success in mapping vision and text to shared latent spaces. Nevertheless, extending these alignment frameworks to three-dimensional (3D) geometric representations has remained a considerable open problem given the inherent topological dissimilarity and non-Euclidean properties of 3D point clouds when compared to language structures.

This Master's thesis provides a thorough empirical and theoretical investigation into cross-modal latent alignment between uni-modal 3D objects and text latent spaces. We train a system on a large scale dataset of 3D objects and their corresponding textual descriptions, extracts features across several multi-modal backbones such as PointNet, OpenCLIP, RoBERTa, and BERT to characterize modalities into high dimensional global embedding.

Firstly, we conduct a strict replication and verification of the state-of-the-art supervised linear alignment framework \textcite{hadgi2025escaping}. An in-depth series of anchor pairs are projected to a common low dimensional shared space through an exact SVD-based Canonical Correlation Analysis (CCA), and subsequently an affine least-squares solution to derive a robust benchmark for this problem. Tested on a hold-out query set, averaged over multiple random initializations, our replication of this system confirms that supervised linear transformations effectively expose shared geometric underlying structures between the modalities.

Secondly, we extensively explore unsupervised and weakly-supervised Optimal Transport (OT) based alignment frameworks including the use of Gromov-Wasserstein (GW) distance metrics \cite{vayer2019optimal}. We analyze the performance of various GW variants such as Sliced GW, Rotation-Invariant SGW, Semi-Supervised RISGW, Fused GW and Low-Rank GW, integrating with recent improvements on intra-domain scale-matching normalization presented by \textcite{li2026scale}. Our results in extensive independent ablation studies demonstrate that all unsupervised GW approaches underperformed consistently at aligning latent spaces with random retrieval rates on all metrics.

Thirdly, we explain these failed alignment outcomes using a theoretical and structural analysis approach. Through the examination of transport plan entropy, mass concentration ratio, and self-structural cost evaluation metrics, we prove that 3D geometric point cloud spaces and text latent spaces fundamentally violate the inherent assumption of structural isometry. Because intra-domain distributions lack any form ofisometric correlation, the quadratic solvers for unsupervised optimal transport are stuck at non-informative and uniform local minima, demonstrating that explicitly based on anchor supervision is critical for successful cross-modal 3D-text alignment, and marking the mathematical limitations of relational optimal transport in multimodal learning.
