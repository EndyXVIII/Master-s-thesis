\chapter*{Summary}
\markboth{Summary}{Summary}

\begingroup
\setlength{\parindent}{0pt}
\setlength{\parskip}{0.8em}

Over the past several years, multimodal artificial intelligence has seen a
substantial rise in its success in mapping vision and text to shared latent
spaces. Nevertheless, extending these alignment frameworks to
three-dimensional (3D) geometric representations has remained a considerable
open problem given the inherent topological dissimilarity and non-Euclidean
properties of 3D point clouds when compared to language structures.

This Master's thesis provides a thorough empirical and theoretical
investigation into cross-modal latent alignment between uni-modal 3D objects
and text latent spaces. We extract features from a large-scale dataset of
3D objects and their corresponding textual descriptions using several
pre-trained encoder backbones --- PointNet, OpenCLIP, RoBERTa, and BERT ---
characterising each modality as a high-dimensional global embedding.

Firstly, we conduct a strict replication and verification of the
state-of-the-art supervised linear alignment framework of
\textcite{hadgi2025escaping}. A set of anchor pairs is projected into a
common low-dimensional shared space through an exact SVD-based Canonical
Correlation Analysis (CCA), followed by an affine least-squares mapping to
derive a robust benchmark. Tested on a held-out query set and averaged over
multiple random seeds, our replication confirms that supervised linear
transformations effectively expose shared geometric structures between the
modalities, achieving $35.7\%$ Top-5 retrieval accuracy on the PointNet
$\times$ CLIP pair.

Secondly, we extensively explore unsupervised and weakly-supervised Optimal
Transport (OT) based alignment frameworks, including the use of
Gromov-Wasserstein (GW) distance metrics \cite{vayer2019optimal}. We analyse
the performance of five GW variants --- Sliced GW, Rotation-Invariant SGW,
Semi-Supervised RISGW, Fused GW, and Low-Rank GW --- integrating the
intra-domain scale-matching normalisation of \textcite{li2026scale}. Through
thirteen independent ablation experiments, we demonstrate that all
unsupervised GW approaches fail consistently, achieving retrieval rates
indistinguishable from random guessing across all metrics. As an original
contribution, we also implement and evaluate two supervised non-linear
extensions: Kernel CCA with Nystr\"{o}m approximation (15.8\% Top-5) and
Riemannian Metric Learning (27.2\% Top-5), the latter reaching 76\% of the
supervised linear baseline performance.

Thirdly, we explain these failed alignment outcomes through a theoretical and
structural analysis. Through the examination of transport plan entropy, mass
concentration ratios, and self-structural GW cost ratios, we prove that 3D
geometric point cloud spaces and text latent spaces fundamentally violate the
assumption of structural isometry. Because the intra-domain distributions
lack isometric correlation, the quadratic solvers for unsupervised optimal
transport converge to non-informative, near-uniform local minima,
demonstrating that explicit anchor supervision is critical for successful
cross-modal 3D-text alignment and marking the mathematical limitations of
relational optimal transport in multimodal learning.
