\chapter*{Summary}
\markboth{Summary}{Summary}

\begingroup
\setlength{\parindent}{0pt}
\setlength{\parskip}{0.8em}

Multimodal artificial intelligence has made real progress over the past several years, particularly at mapping vision and text into shared latent spaces. Extending that success to three-dimensional geometric representations, though, has proven much harder --- 3D point clouds are topologically dissimilar and non-Euclidean in ways that language structures simply aren't, and that mismatch has kept 3D-text alignment a genuinely open problem.

This Master's thesis takes on that problem directly, with a thorough empirical and theoretical look at cross-modal latent alignment between uni-modal 3D objects and text. We extract features from a large-scale dataset of 3D objects and their textual descriptions using several pre-trained backbones --- PointNet, OpenCLIP, \gls{roberta}, and \gls{bert} --- treating each modality as a high-dimensional global embedding.

We start by strictly replicating and verifying \textcite{hadgi2025escaping}'s state-of-the-art supervised linear alignment framework. Anchor pairs get projected into a shared, low-dimensional space through an exact \gls{svd}-based \gls{cca}, followed by an affine least-squares mapping, giving us a solid benchmark to work from. Tested on a held-out query set and averaged over multiple random seeds, our replication confirms that supervised linear transformations really do expose shared geometric structure between the two modalities --- reaching $35.7\%$ Top-5 retrieval accuracy on the PointNet $\times$ \gls{clip} pair.

From there, we turn to unsupervised and weakly-supervised \gls{ot}-based alignment, including \gls{gw} distance metrics \cite{vayer2019optimal}. We test five GW variants --- \gls{sgw}, \gls{risgw}, \gls{ssrisgw}, \gls{fgw}, and \gls{lrgw} --- combined with \textcite{li2026scale}'s intra-domain scale-matching normalisation. None of it works. Across thirteen independent ablation experiments, every unsupervised GW approach fails consistently, landing at retrieval rates indistinguishable from random guessing on every metric we track. As an original contribution beyond the paper's scope, we also implement and evaluate two supervised non-linear extensions: \gls{kcca} with Nystr\"{o}m approximation, which reaches $15.8\%$ Top-5, and \gls{rml}, which does considerably better at $27.2\%$ --- about 76\% of what the supervised linear baseline achieves.

Finally, we work out why the alignment fails, through a theoretical and structural analysis. Looking at transport plan entropy, mass concentration ratios, and self-structural GW cost ratios, we show that 3D point cloud spaces and text latent spaces fundamentally violate the assumption of structural isometry. Because the intra-domain distributions simply aren't isometrically correlated, the quadratic solvers behind unsupervised optimal transport converge to non-informative, near-uniform local minima almost by necessity. The upshot is that explicit anchor supervision isn't just helpful but essentially required for successful cross-modal 3D-text alignment --- which marks out a fairly clear mathematical limit on what relational optimal transport can do in multimodal learning.
